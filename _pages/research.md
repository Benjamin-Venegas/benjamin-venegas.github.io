<!-- Finite Element Systems  (FES)
      A categoric theoretical approach to finite elements, proposed by [Snorre Christiansen](https://www.mn.uio.no/math/english/people/aca/snorrec/index.html).  The main idea is that assigning a FE space to each cell of the underlying cellular complex (mesh) gives a combinatorial structure, which helps one to prove results and constructing objects such as interpolators by means of induction on the dimension of cells. This helps to acknowledge that some usual constructions in finite elements, such as gluing local spaces to obtain global FE spaces or the traditional unisolvency, are particular instances of more general procedures known in the categorical setting. The local-to-global passage in the former example concerns with the notion of sheaves and, more concretely, a glued space is an instance of a projective/inverse limit.

      Any cellular complex $\mathcal T$ can be regarded as a category whose objects are the cells (of all dimensions) and the morphisms are the inclusion maps between cells. In this situation, the aforementioned assignment of local spaces to each cell defines a contravariant functor from the category of complexes of vector spaces to the category defined by $\mathcal T$, we called this a FES functor. The contravariance ensures that restriction to subcells commutes with the differential of the complex, which we know to hold for the trace and exterior derivative of differential forms. A FES is then the image of a chosen complex via the FES functor.
